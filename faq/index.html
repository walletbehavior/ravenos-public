What is RavenOS?

RavenOS is a market intelligence platform built around structure, pressure, replay, participation, rotation, and market context.

The goal is not to tell users what to buy or sell. The goal is to help users understand what is happening, why it is happening, how similar it is to prior market structures, which conditions support the current environment, and which conditions could cause it to fail.

RavenOS launches with crypto and perpetual futures as its deepest supported markets, but the architecture is designed to support additional asset classes over time.

What makes RavenOS different?

Most platforms focus on price, indicators, news, funding rates, open interest, and social activity.

RavenOS focuses on market structure, participant behavior, pressure formation, replay analysis, outcome research, and capital rotation. The question is not only what price is doing. The question is what kind of structure is forming.

Does RavenOS provide trade signals?

No. RavenOS does not provide buy recommendations, sell recommendations, financial advice, investment management, brokerage services, or trade execution. RavenOS provides research, analytics, context, and market intelligence.

What is Flow Score?

Flow Score is a RavenOS summary metric that combines multiple market characteristics into a single structure score.

It may consider participation, attention, liquidity, breadth, replay, pressure, and outcome quality. Flow Score is not a trade signal. It is a summary of market structure.

What is Pressure?

Pressure describes how market forces are building within a structure. States may include Constructive, Elevated, Crowded, Exhausted, and Unstable.

Pressure can come from funding, open interest, participation, liquidity, volume, and positioning. Pressure is one component of market context.

What is Replay?

Replay answers: have we seen this before?

Raven compares current structures against previously observed structures to identify similar conditions, environments, and market behaviors. Replay does not predict the future. It provides historical context.

What are Replay Outcomes?

Replay Outcomes answer: what usually happened next?

For similar historical structures, Raven may summarize expansion, continuation, reversal, and failure. Replay Outcomes are research tools, not forecasts.

What is Structure?

Structure describes the condition of a market. Examples include participation broadening, participation narrowing, pressure expanding, pressure weakening, liquidity improving, and liquidity deteriorating.

Structure is often more important than direction.

What is Structure Lab?

Structure Lab is RavenOS research infrastructure. It studies setup families, replay bands, failure modes, outcome distributions, symbol concentration, and structure quality.

Structure Lab is designed to help users understand which structures historically separated from noise.

What are Setup Families?

Setup families are recurring market structures identified by Raven research. Examples may include Compression Release, Participant Rotation, Volatility Squeeze, and OI Contraction.

These are research concepts, not trading recommendations.

What is Fresh Survival?

Fresh Survival measures whether new activity continues to survive over time. It asks whether new participants are staying active, liquidity is persisting, attention is fading, and outcomes are improving.

Fresh Survival is especially useful in emerging markets and smaller capitalization assets.

What is Attention Velocity?

Attention Velocity measures how quickly market attention is changing. States may include Exploding, Rising, Stable, Fading, and Collapsing.

Attention alone is not enough. Raven evaluates attention alongside participation and structure.

What is Participation?

Participation measures how broadly activity is distributed. Examples include broad participation, narrow participation, expanding participation, and contracting participation.

Participation often influences structure quality.

What is Participant Intelligence?

Participant Intelligence studies who appears to be influencing market structure. Participant categories may include Smart Money, Retail, Market Makers, and Unknown.

Raven focuses on aggregate behavior rather than copying individual participants.

What is Liquidity Attraction?

Liquidity Attraction measures how strongly current structures appear connected to nearby liquidity zones. States may include Weak, Moderate, Strong, and Extreme.

Liquidity Attraction is used as context rather than prediction.

What is Coverage?

Coverage describes how much Raven understands about a market. Public means mostly public market data. Indexed means Raven has accumulated meaningful observations. Deep Raven means Raven has extensive historical context and structure history.

Coverage is not a quality score. It describes research depth.

What is Confidence?

Confidence describes how much supporting evidence exists behind an observation. It may consider sample depth, replay quality, data freshness, coverage quality, and outcome history.

Examples include Low, Developing, Moderate, and High. Confidence is not certainty.

What are Heatmaps?

Heatmaps visualize market structure across groups of assets. They may focus on market capitalization, chains, sectors, perpetual futures, and rotation.

Heatmaps are designed to help users identify emerging changes in participation and structure.

What is the Degen Terminal?

The Degen Terminal is RavenOS's crypto discovery environment. It focuses on survival, participation, rotation, replay, and attention.

Rather than asking what is up, the Degen Terminal asks what is becoming important.

What are Raven Cap Buckets?

Raven uses behavioral market-cap buckets:

  • Nano: under $100k
  • Micro: $100k - $1M
  • Small: $1M - $10M
  • Mid: $10M - $100M
  • Large: $100M - $1B
  • Mega: above $1B

Each bucket behaves differently and is analyzed separately.

What is the Perps Terminal?

The Perps Terminal focuses on leveraged markets. Topics include pressure, positioning, liquidity, replay, and outcome conditions.

The Perps Terminal is designed to study market structure rather than generate trade signals.

What is Atlas?

Atlas is a separate high-tier RavenOS product focused on market regime, liquidity conditions, breadth quality, volatility environment, and cross-market context.

Atlas is designed for users who need high-level context rather than individual asset analysis.

What are Watchlists?

Watchlists allow users to track instruments and monitor changes in structure. Watchlists integrate with alerts, structure changes, replay changes, and coverage changes.

What are Alerts?

Alerts notify users when important market structure changes occur, such as pressure changes, replay changes, structure changes, and participation changes.

Alerts are informational. They are not trade recommendations.

Is RavenOS AI?

RavenOS uses models, analytics, and research systems internally. However, RavenOS should not be viewed as an AI trading signal platform.

The focus is research, structure, context, and explanation.

Does RavenOS guarantee profits?

No. RavenOS does not guarantee profits, performance, returns, or trade outcomes. RavenOS provides information and research. Users remain responsible for their own decisions.

Who is RavenOS for?

RavenOS is designed for traders, researchers, analysts, builders, market operators, and anyone who wants to understand market behavior more deeply than traditional dashboards allow.

What is the long-term vision?

RavenOS aims to become a market intelligence operating system. The objective is to help users understand structure, pressure, participation, replay, rotation, and regime across multiple markets and timeframes.

The goal is not prediction. The goal is understanding.
