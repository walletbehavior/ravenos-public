01

Volatility, liquidity, manipulation, and total loss

Digital assets can move violently, trade around the clock, become illiquid, lose all economic value, or cease trading. Small-cap, newly launched, thinly held, tokenized, wrapped, synthetic, bridged, and leveraged products can present substantially greater risk. Markets may be concentrated, opaque, manipulated, wash traded, or controlled by insiders or automated actors.

An apparent balance, market capitalization, last trade, or mark does not mean you can sell at that value. Available liquidity, order-book depth, pool reserves, transfer rules, route access, gas, and market conditions determine what may be executable. There may be no buyer, no exit route, or no practical recovery after a mistake or fraud.

02

Data, Raven analytics, and uncertainty

Market data can be delayed, sampled, stale, missing, contradictory, mis-decoded, incorrectly mapped, or unavailable. Providers may use different definitions for price, liquidity, holders, volume, supply, token age, graduation, and market capitalization. A chart and headline metric may cover different venues or timestamps unless RavenOS explicitly binds them.

Raven Reads, wallet screening, technical marks, risk indicators, cohorts, classifications, rankings, copyability, alerts, and Portfolio Governor decisions are analytical outputs based on available evidence and assumptions. “Available,” “executable,” “verified,” “high confidence,” or “low risk” are bounded product states, not assurances that an asset is safe, lawful, appropriate, profitable, or immune from failure.

Unknown is not zero. RavenOS may refuse or mark a conclusion indeterminate when price, liquidity, cost basis, gas, authority, finality, route, balance, or provider health is unresolved. You should independently verify facts material to your decision.

03

Quotes, approvals, fills, and irreversible transactions

A quote is an estimate for a specific chain, venue, asset identity, amount, wallet, and short time window. It may expire or become materially worse before signing. Spread, price impact, slippage, precision, routing, fees, and available balance can change. A preview or successful simulation does not guarantee execution.

Wallet approvals can authorize contracts to spend tokens. Review the spender, amount, expiration, chain, and transaction data. Unlimited or malicious approvals can expose assets. An order or transaction may fail, remain pending, be replaced, execute partially, execute at the edge of the allowed price, or return an ambiguous response requiring reconciliation.

Blockchain transfers are generally irreversible. An incorrect address, contract, token, network, amount, memo, or approval can cause permanent loss. RavenOS cannot promise that a protocol, validator, sequencer, venue, wallet, bridge, router, or counterparty will reverse or reimburse a transaction.

04

Token and smart-contract risk

Smart contracts can contain defects, malicious logic, economic exploits, unsafe dependencies, governance attacks, oracle failures, or upgrade paths. Audits, verified source, age, liquidity locks, holder count, or prior successful use do not eliminate risk.

Token controllers may be able to mint, burn, pause, freeze, blacklist, upgrade, tax, restrict, or redirect transfers; change supply; remove liquidity; or exercise admin powers through a proxy or hidden relationship. Sell simulation can become invalid after a contract or state change. A “honeypot” check cannot prove future sellability.

Symbol and name are not identity. Same-symbol assets on different chains, native and wrapped assets, tokenized equities and underlying equities, and canonical and bridged stablecoins are different instruments. Verify the exact mint or contract and chain before signing.

05

Networks, finality, congestion, and MEV

Blockchains and venues differ in confirmation, finality, sequencer, validator, gas, fee, reorganization, outage, and censorship behavior. A transaction visible as pending or confirmed may later fail, be dropped, replaced, reordered, or reorganized. Provider disagreement may prevent RavenOS from determining the current state.

Congestion can increase gas, priority fees, and latency. Searchers, validators, block builders, sequencers, and other actors may observe and reorder transactions. Front-running, sandwiching, back-running, adverse selection, and information leakage can worsen execution. Private or protected routing, when available, does not eliminate these risks.

RPC, indexer, WebSocket, sequencer, wallet, and API failures can delay or prevent quotes, signing, submission, status, and reconciliation. RavenOS may fail closed, but fail-closed controls cannot restore a missed market or reverse an already submitted transaction.

06

External and embedded wallets

You are responsible for protecting wallet access, authentication factors, recovery methods, connected devices, and destination addresses. Phishing, malware, malicious browser extensions, SIM or email compromise, social engineering, and provider-account takeover can lead to loss.

External wallet compatibility and recovery are controlled by the wallet provider and your keys. RavenOS cannot recover a seed phrase or private key it does not possess. Disconnecting a wallet from the interface does not revoke onchain token approvals.

Optional Privy embedded wallets use separate wallet infrastructure. Export, recovery, signing, and user-control behavior must be tested and disclosed for each supported ecosystem before broad launch. If delegated signing is later activated, it introduces additional authorization and compromise risk and requires a separate, explicit policy-bound approval.

07

Universal USDC, stablecoins, and cross-chain routes

A displayed aggregate USDC amount is not one instantly available balance. USDC and similarly named tokens can exist as different issuer, native, wrapped, or bridged representations on separate networks. Capital on Ethereum cannot satisfy a Solana or Robinhood Chain order until a separate route settles.

Stablecoins can depeg, be frozen, fail, lose reserves, become unredeemable, or trade at a discount. Bridges and cross-chain solvers add contract, validator, message, liquidity, relayer, route, counterparty, finality, and delay risk. Source departure and destination arrival are separate events; a transfer may be delayed or stranded.

Cross-chain transactions can require source and destination gas, additional approvals, bridge-native fees, and multiple signatures. A limit order can miss its market while funds move. RavenOS must not describe bridging as atomic, instant, or risk-free, and automated bridging is not active in the audited implementation.

08

Perpetuals, leverage, margin, and venue risk

Perpetual futures and other leveraged products can create losses greater and faster than unleveraged spot positions. Funding, mark price, oracle price, liquidation thresholds, margin mode, position size, maintenance requirements, and venue rules can change. Liquidation can occur before you can respond.

Hyperliquid and any other venue operate under their own technical and legal terms. Builder-fee approval is a separate wallet authorization; fee caps and venue constraints vary between spot and perpetual products. A position, balance, and USDC settlement asset on Hyperliquid are not interchangeable with onchain USDC elsewhere.

RavenOS must not enable autonomous securities, options, or other restricted instrument execution merely because a technical adapter exists. Legal eligibility and venue access require separate review.

09

Liquidity provision, lending, and yield

Liquidity providers face impermanent loss, inventory drift, concentrated-range inactivity, adverse selection, smart-contract and oracle failures, token collapse, liquidity removal, pool migration, fee changes, and gas costs. Borrowing and lending introduce interest-rate, liquidation, collateral, protocol, and counterparty risk.

A displayed annualized yield, fee APR, historical performance, or incentive rate is not guaranteed. Short observation periods and token incentives can exaggerate a rate. Historical fees differ from executable future returns. RavenOS LP intelligence is analysis; live LP execution is not activated by this disclosure.

10

Raven and third-party costs

When enabled, Raven’s native spot and Copy execution fee is 1.00% for Standard, paid Pro, and Pro trial accounts. Active Pro earns 30% of Raven’s actual reconciled fee back in USDC-denominated rewards; the estimate is not available before fee confirmation. Hyperliquid is excluded from this cashback program. Hyperliquid rules may differ by instrument because the venue constrains builder fees. The confirmation should show the exact Raven fee actually embedded or applied; a product-level policy alone is not proof of collection.

Network fees, priority fees, protocol or venue fees, router or solver fees, bridge fees, funding, spread, slippage, price impact, token taxes, and other costs are separate. Fees can make a profitable source trade unprofitable for a follower or make a small transaction uneconomic.

11

Regulatory, geographic, sanctions, and tax risk

Laws, rules, enforcement positions, token classifications, and venue eligibility can change rapidly. An asset or activity available technically may be restricted legally. Tokenized equities, securities, derivatives, commodities, payments, custody, money transmission, promotion, and automated activity may carry additional requirements.

You must not use RavenOS to evade sanctions, geographic restrictions, market rules, tax obligations, or provider eligibility. RavenOS may restrict assets, chains, instruments, accounts, or regions while a compliance question is unresolved.

Transactions, rewards, fees, funding, bridging, LP activity, airdrops, and referrals may create reporting or tax obligations. Records can be incomplete. Consult qualified advisers for your circumstances.

12

Proposed first-trade acknowledgement

Before first live trading, the product should require an unchecked, server-recorded acknowledgement bound to this exact version and content hash. Acceptance should not repeat on every trade, but each confirmation must still show transaction-specific facts.

Proposed acknowledgement: I understand that digital assets and leveraged products can lose all value; Raven data, simulations, quotes, and risk checks can be incomplete or wrong; a quote is not a fill; blockchain transactions may be irreversible; and I remain responsible for reviewing and authorizing each action.

Accepting this disclosure does not waive rights that cannot lawfully be waived and does not by itself authorize RavenOS to sign, submit, copy, bridge, or trade.
